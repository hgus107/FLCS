# Consumer Lending and Credit Assessment Policy

Document reference: FLCS-LND-001
Owner: Credit Risk
Review cycle: annual

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs the assessment of consumer loan applications, the affordability tests applied, and the circumstances in which an application must be referred rather than decided. It applies to all credit analysts and to any agent producing a recommendation on their behalf. Commercial lending and mortgage lending are out of scope and are governed separately.

## 2. Recommendations, not decisions

An analyst or agent produces a recommendation of approve, decline, or refer. A recommendation is not a decision. Only an authorised credit officer may record a decision against an application, and no system may present a recommendation to a customer as though it were an outcome.

Every recommendation must state the reasoning and the specific figures relied on. A recommendation that cites a conclusion without the underlying ratios will fail quality review.

## 3. Credit score bands

Credit scores are assessed on a three hundred to nine hundred scale.

Scores of seven hundred and fifty and above are treated as strong. Applications in this band may be recommended for approval where affordability tests also pass.

Scores between six hundred and fifty and seven hundred and forty nine are acceptable. Approval is available but the affordability tests are applied strictly, with no discretion on marginal cases.

Scores between five hundred and fifty and six hundred and forty nine are weak. These applications are referred for manual underwriting regardless of affordability, because the score indicates prior difficulty that the ratios alone will not reveal.

Scores below five hundred and fifty are treated as adverse. The recommendation is decline unless there are documented exceptional circumstances, which must be recorded in full.

## 4. Affordability tests

Three ratios are calculated for every application. Analysts judge the ratios; they do not recalculate them, because inconsistent arithmetic between analysts is a recurring source of error.

Loan to income is the total advance divided by gross annual income. A ratio at or below two point zero is within appetite. Between two point zero and three point zero the application is referred. Above three point zero the recommendation is decline.

Repayment to income is the annual repayment divided by gross annual income, expressed as a percentage. At or below thirty percent is within appetite. Between thirty and forty five percent the application is referred. Above forty five percent the recommendation is decline, irrespective of credit score or assets.

Assets to loan is total declared assets divided by the advance. A ratio at or above one point zero materially reduces loss given default and may support a referral being resolved in the customer's favour. Assets never override a failed repayment to income test, because assets are not income and cannot service a monthly obligation.

## 5. Term effects

A short term on a large advance produces a high repayment to income ratio even where loan to income appears acceptable. Analysts must read the two ratios together. An application with a loan to income ratio of two point one and a two year term is materially more dangerous than the same advance over fifteen years, and the repayment ratio is the test that will reveal it.

Extending the term to bring an application within appetite is permitted only where the customer has requested it. Analysts must not recommend a term extension solely to make a marginal application passable.

## 6. Prior applications

The applicant's borrowing history must be retrieved before any recommendation. A previous decline is a material fact and must be referenced explicitly in the reasoning.

Where a previous application was declined and the applicant's circumstances have not materially improved, a repeat application within six months is recommended for decline on that basis alone.

Where circumstances have improved, the improvement must be quantified in the reasoning rather than asserted.

## 7. Interaction with financial crime

An open fraud case on the applicant's record requires referral to the fraud team before any lending recommendation is finalised. The credit position is assessed and recorded as normal, but the recommendation is held at refer until the fraud team responds.

This applies even where the credit position is strong. Lending to a customer whose account may be compromised risks advancing funds to a third party, and the credit assessment is silent on that risk.

Evidence of suspicious transaction activity that has not yet resulted in a case must still be recorded in the reasoning and flagged to the fraud team.

## 8. Interaction with compliance

An expired identity document, an unverified address, or a High customer risk rating each require the application to be referred pending compliance clearance.

A sanctions screening match, however weak, requires referral. Analysts must not form their own view on whether a screening match is a false positive.

## 9. Hardship and payment holidays

Customers in financial difficulty may request a payment holiday of up to three months. Interest continues to accrue throughout and the customer must be told so in plain terms.

Eligibility requires the account to be no more than thirty days in arrears and to have at least six months of payment history. A hardship arrangement is recorded on the credit file and will affect future applications.

Applications for hardship from customers with an open fraud case are referred to the fraud team before any arrangement is offered.

## 10. Fairness and prohibited grounds

Recommendations must rest only on the applicant's financial position and credit history. Age, gender, marital status, housing tenure, occupation, number of dependants, and place of residence must not be used as reasons in themselves, even where they appear in the application record.

Where such a field is relied on indirectly, for example dependants as an input to expenditure, the reasoning must state the financial mechanism rather than the characteristic.

## 11. Record keeping

All recommendations, the ratios relied on, and the underlying application data are retained for seven years. Quality review samples five percent of recommendations monthly. A recommendation that omits the applicant's borrowing history, or that cites a ratio without the figure, is recorded as a control failure.
