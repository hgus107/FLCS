# Financial Crime Compliance Policy

Document reference: FLCS-CMP-001
Owner: Compliance
Review cycle: annual

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs customer due diligence, sanctions screening, and the compliance assessment of new product applications. It applies to compliance analysts, onboarding staff, and any agent producing findings on their behalf. Transaction monitoring for fraud is governed separately under the Fraud Policy, though the two functions share findings.

## 2. Know your customer requirements

Every customer must have a current identity record on file before any product is opened. The record comprises identity document type, document number, expiry date, verified residential address, politically exposed person status, and a risk rating.

An identity document past its expiry date invalidates the record. The customer is treated as not verified until a replacement is provided, regardless of how long they have banked with the institution.

An unverified address is a finding in its own right. Address verification is the control that prevents statement redirection, which is a common precursor to account takeover.

## 3. Absence of a record is not a finding

An applicant with no customer relationship will have no KYC record. This is expected and must not be reported as a deficiency. New applicants are onboarded through the standard verification process.

The distinction matters: a missing record on a new applicant is routine, while a missing or lapsed record on an existing customer is a control failure requiring remediation.

## 4. Risk ratings

Customers are rated Low, Medium, or High.

Low rated customers are reviewed every three years. Medium rated customers are reviewed every two years. High rated customers are reviewed annually, and any product application from a High rated customer requires supervisor approval.

A rating is not a judgement about the customer's character and must not be communicated to them as such. It determines review frequency and approval routing, nothing more.

A review date more than one cycle overdue is a control failure and must be reported even where every other element of the record is current.

## 5. Politically exposed persons

A politically exposed person holds or has held a prominent public function, or is an immediate family member or close associate of someone who does.

PEP status is not a reason to decline. It requires enhanced due diligence: senior management approval, establishment of source of wealth, and annual review irrespective of the customer's risk rating.

PEP status must be re-checked at every review, because the customer's circumstances may have changed since onboarding.

## 6. Sanctions screening

Every applicant name is screened against the consolidated sanctions list before any product is opened, and every existing customer is rescreened whenever the list is updated.

Screening is fuzzy by design. Sanctioned parties deliberately vary spelling, transliteration, and word order, so exact matching alone would miss most genuine hits. The consequence is a high volume of false positives, and this is expected rather than a defect.

A similarity score is a measure of textual resemblance. It is not a probability that the customer is sanctioned. A score of one point zero means the strings are identical, which most commonly indicates two unrelated people sharing a common name.

## 7. Handling a screening match

An analyst must never record a customer as sanctioned on the strength of a name match. The match is a trigger for verification, not a conclusion.

Verification requires comparison of secondary identifiers: date of birth, nationality, place of birth, and known aliases held on the sanctions record. Where those identifiers are absent from the sanctions entry, the match is escalated to the Sanctions team rather than resolved locally.

Where a match is confirmed, the account is frozen immediately, no funds are released, and the matter is reported to the relevant authority within the statutory period. The customer must not be told why.

Where a match is discounted, the reasoning and the identifiers compared are recorded, and the name is added to the local whitelist so the same false positive is not re-investigated at every screening.

## 8. Suspicious activity reporting

Cash transactions at or above ten thousand dollars require a currency transaction report.

A pattern of transactions immediately below that threshold is treated as possible structuring and reported regardless of the individual amounts.

Compliance staff must never disclose that a report has been filed or contemplated. Tipping off is a criminal offence and applies to any communication with the customer, including hints, delays explained by reference to the report, or advice to withdraw an application.

## 9. Account changes

Any change to address, telephone number, or email requires two factor verification.

Where the identity document on file has expired, no account change may be processed until it is refreshed. This is absolute and there is no discretion.

Changes of contact details for High rated customers require supervisor approval, because contact takeover is a common precursor to account fraud.

## 10. Escalation

Findings are escalated to a compliance manager where a sanctions match cannot be discounted locally, where a customer is identified as a PEP after onboarding, where a review is more than one cycle overdue, or where the same finding recurs on the same customer within twelve months.

Escalations are raised the same working day. A finding held overnight without escalation is a control failure.

## 11. Record keeping

Due diligence records, screening results including discounted matches, and the reasoning for every decision are retained for six years after the relationship ends.

Quality review samples ten percent of onboarding files monthly. A discounted screening match without recorded identifier comparison, or a processed account change against an expired document, is recorded as a control failure and reported to the audit committee.
