# Customer Support Policy

Document reference: FLCS-SUP-001
Owner: Customer Operations
Review cycle: annual

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy governs how customer enquiries, disputes, and complaints are handled. It applies to support agents and to any automated agent answering a customer or an internal user on their behalf. Where a support matter touches fraud, lending, or compliance, the relevant specialist policy takes precedence and this document defers to it.

## 2. Disputed card transactions

A customer may dispute a card transaction within sixty days of the statement date on which it appeared. Disputes raised outside that window are considered on their merits but carry no automatic right to provisional credit.

Provisional credit is applied within ten business days of the dispute being raised. The customer is told in plain language that the credit is provisional and may be reversed.

Investigations complete within forty five days for domestic transactions and ninety days for cross border transactions. Where the deadline will be missed, the customer receives a holding response explaining why before the deadline passes, not after.

Where the merchant provides compelling evidence the transaction was authorised, provisional credit is reversed with five business days written notice. The evidence must be summarised for the customer; a bare statement that the dispute failed is not sufficient.

## 3. Disputes distinguished from fraud

A dispute concerns a transaction the customer recognises but contests, such as goods not received, a duplicate charge, or a cancelled subscription still billing.

Fraud concerns a transaction the customer does not recognise at all.

Agents must establish which applies before proceeding, because the two follow different processes, different liability rules, and different deadlines. Misrouting a fraud report as a dispute delays the block on the card and increases loss.

## 4. Lost or stolen cards

A lost or stolen card is blocked immediately on report, by phone or in the app, without waiting for identity verification to complete.

A replacement is despatched within three business days, or the next business day for premium accounts.

The customer is liable for a maximum of fifty dollars of unauthorised use occurring before the report, and nothing after it. Where the bank's own monitoring detected the fraud first, there is no liability at all.

## 5. Card blocks

Agents may lift a soft block once the customer has confirmed identity through two factors.

Agents may not lift any block on a card with an open fraud case. That requires fraud team sign off, and the agent tells the customer that the review is with a specialist team rather than speculating about the outcome.

A hard block cannot be lifted. The card is reissued.

## 6. Fees

The late payment fee is thirty five dollars. The foreign transaction fee is three percent of the transaction value. The cash advance fee is five percent, with a minimum of ten dollars.

A first late payment fee may be waived once per rolling twelve months, at agent discretion, for accounts in good standing. The waiver is recorded so the twelve month window can be enforced.

Fees on an account with an open fraud case are held rather than charged, and released or cancelled when the case closes. A customer must not be pursued for a fee arising from a transaction they did not make.

## 7. Complaints

Every complaint is acknowledged within one business day.

Resolution is within fifteen business days. Where that will be missed, a holding response is issued explaining the delay and giving a revised date.

A complaint is escalated to a team leader where the customer asks for it, where the claim exceeds five thousand dollars, or where the subject is a regulated matter such as a lending decision or sanctions screening.

Customers are told of their right to refer the matter to the ombudsman if it remains unresolved after eight weeks. This is stated proactively, not only when asked.

## 8. What agents must not disclose

Agents must never confirm or deny that a suspicious activity report has been filed or considered. This is tipping off and is a criminal offence.

Agents must not disclose a customer's internal risk rating, the detection rules that flagged a transaction, or the contents of a fraud case file.

Where a customer presses for detail the agent cannot give, the agent says plainly that the information is not something they are able to share, and does not invent an alternative explanation.

## 9. Identity verification before disclosure

No account information is disclosed until identity is verified to the standard for the request. Balance and transaction detail require two factors. Any change to contact details requires two factors and, where the identity document on file has expired, cannot be processed at all.

Inbound calls are never trusted on the strength of the number displayed.

## 10. Vulnerable customers

Where a customer discloses bereavement, serious illness, financial hardship, or coercion, the agent records the disclosure and adapts the interaction: no upselling, no time pressure, and an offer to route the matter to the specialist support team.

Suspected coercion or financial abuse is escalated the same day and must not be discussed with any third party on the account.

## 11. Tone and accuracy

Answers are given in plain language, without internal jargon, policy reference numbers, or system names.

An agent who does not know the answer says so and escalates. Guessing at a policy position is treated as a control failure, because a customer acting on a wrong answer suffers real detriment.

## 12. Record keeping

Every interaction, the verification performed, and the outcome are recorded at the time. Complaints and their resolutions are retained for six years.

Quality review samples five percent of interactions monthly. A disclosure made without adequate verification, or a fee waived outside the twelve month window, is recorded as a control failure.
